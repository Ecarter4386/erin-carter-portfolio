From Manual Product Entry to a Governed Commerce System

A Product Owner case study focused on product information architecture, workflow governance, and responsible AI-assisted commerce operations.

The operating problem

A luxury multi-brand catalog depended on inconsistent vendor data, repeated manual writing, fragmented image production, and product-by-product decisions. Information was researched, interpreted, and rewritten separately for PDPs, collections, SEO, alt text, campaigns, feeds, and uploads.

The catalog could continue growing, but the operating model could not. The goal was to capture verified product truth once and make it reusable across the entire digital ecosystem.

Translating business needs into system requirements

Customer and merchandising needs

  • Clear fit, material, construction, care, and styling information
  • Consistent classification across designers and categories
  • Accurate collections, search terms, and filter behavior

Operational needs

  • Reduce repeated research and rewriting
  • Support bulk publishing and continued catalog growth
  • Define human review, QA, and exception handling

The governed product record

I designed a structured record that separated factual product truth from customer-facing presentation.

01

Core identity

Designer, title, category, color, season, style identifiers.

02

Material + construction

Composition, trim, lining, closure, transparency, care.

03

Fit + silhouette

Fit classification, length, rise, waist, sleeves, neckline.

04

Distribution

PDPs, collections, search, SEO, alt text, feeds, and campaigns.

Structure the facts that must remain consistent. Preserve flexibility where human interpretation adds customer value.

Intake-to-publication workflow

The system moved through eight governed stages:

Intake → research → verification → structured entry → draft → human review → Shopify implementation → prepublication QA

Facts were treated as verified, observable, or unsupported. Unsupported information was excluded rather than converted into plausible-sounding product copy.

Automation boundaries

AI and automation supported

  • Initial field mapping
  • Controlled-value recommendations
  • Description, metadata, and alt-text drafts
  • Bulk-file preparation and repetitive transformations

Human owners retained

  • Source credibility and fact verification
  • Fit, material, and construction interpretation
  • Luxury editorial judgment and image accuracy
  • Exceptions, edge cases, and final approval
Automation could accelerate a verified process. It could not replace verification.

Business value

  • Created a repeatable product intake and publishing model across designers and source formats
  • Reduced one-off decisions by reusing verified information across downstream outputs
  • Improved consistency between written content, visual representation, search, and merchandising
  • Established the structured inputs and governance required for responsible automation
  • Converted recurring corrections into documented system rules and QA standards

No unsupported time-savings percentages are claimed. Outcomes are described through documented structural and operational changes.
