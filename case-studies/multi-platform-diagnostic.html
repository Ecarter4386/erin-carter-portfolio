Overview

A luxury retail commerce launch had remained blocked despite years of prior work. The visible symptom was “the site cannot launch,” but the underlying system included three overlapping platforms, unclear ownership, incomplete product data, an underestimated catalog, and technical defects that silently damaged records downstream.

The engagement required fast diagnosis under a fixed timeline: make the real system visible, stop treating the work as one undifferentiated launch, and leave behind a path the client could operate.

Diagnostic findings

  • Source-of-truth ambiguity: three platforms contained overlapping product and operational data, but no system clearly governed the complete record.
  • Scope mismatch: the catalog was dramatically larger and more complex than the engagement had been framed to support.
  • Silent handle collisions: distinct products could be assigned conflicting identifiers, causing records to merge at the database level.
  • Dirty upstream inputs: inconsistent naming, missing fields, and duplicate logic created errors at every downstream step.
  • Launch risk on mobile: the customer experience needed technical attention before a high-volume catalog could be responsibly released.

Reframing the engagement

I replaced the idea of one monolithic launch with a phased operating model:

01

Infrastructure repair

Clarify ownership, data flow, technical defects, and import controls.

02

Priority onboarding

Prepare the highest-value brands and validate the process before scaling.

03

Catalog expansion

Move the remaining assortment through the validated pipeline.

04

Ongoing operations

Document intake, QA, and ownership so the launch could be maintained.

Solution designed

Pre-import product-data pipeline

Created spreadsheet-based controls to normalize product records, detect handle collisions, and resolve conflicts before data entered Shopify.

Repeatable intake and QA

Documented a pre-import checklist and staged review process so each new batch followed the same standards rather than relying on memory.

AI-assisted image proof of concept

Architected and validated a controlled workflow for a made-to-order product line, with defined source inputs, image-generation steps, QA criteria, and human approval gates.

Decision-ready scope

Translated technical findings into a phased implementation plan the client could budget, prioritize, and continue beyond the engagement.

Outcome

  • Made the true catalog scope and platform dependencies visible before additional launch risk accumulated
  • Prevented a silent data defect from continuing through future product imports
  • Converted an unbounded deliverable into an executable sequence of work
  • Left repeatable controls for intake, validation, import, and quality review
  • Demonstrated a viable AI-assisted production workflow without removing human accountability
